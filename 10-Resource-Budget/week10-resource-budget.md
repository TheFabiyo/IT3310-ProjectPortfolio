## Estimation Approach

I used a combination of bottom-up and parametric estimating. I reviewed my Week 8 schedule to identify the work each role would be responsible for and estimated how much time they would need. I then multiplied their hours by their estimated hourly rates.

I assumed the Project Manager would work eight hours per week throughout the 39-week project. If that increased by two hours per week, the cost would rise by $5,850.

I also assumed the Developer would work 16 weeks at 30 hours per week. Two additional weeks at the same schedule would increase labor costs by $5,700.

These estimates may need to be revised if the team encounters unexpected technical problems or changes to the schedule.

## Budget Tradeoff Analysis

If the sponsor requested a 10% reduction, the budget would need to decrease by $10,119.50.

I would first review staffing hours to determine whether any tasks or meetings could be handled more efficiently. Reducing Project Manager hours could save money but might create communication problems between the clinics.

I would also review software and infrastructure costs to see whether we could use existing resources. However, reducing these expenses too much could create problems with testing and system reliability.

I would not reduce critical testing, patient data validation, or necessary training to reach the budget target. I would only recommend changes that still support the project's approved outcome.
